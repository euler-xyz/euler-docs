# Euler Protocol Risk Disclosures

## I. Introduction

**Purpose**: This Risk Disclosures document is designed to inform users of the potential risks associated with using the Euler protocol. It aims to provide clarity on the inherent uncertainties and responsibilities involved in interacting with decentralised finance (DeFi) platforms.

**Scope**: The disclosures outlined herein apply to all interactions with the Euler protocol, including the use of its smart contracts, participation in its governance, and engagement with its associated services.

**General:** The Euler protocol is a decentralised finance (DeFi) application designed to provide financial services without relying on traditional intermediaries. By engaging with the protocol, you acknowledge and accept that you are participating in a highly experimental and rapidly evolving space. The risks associated with DeFi are significant, and you should only use the protocol if you fully understand these risks and can bear the potential losses. Under no circumstances will Euler be liable for any loss or damage resulting from your use of the protocol.

- The Euler protocol and all related software, including blockchain technology and smart contracts, are experimental and in continuous development. The protocol is provided "as is" and "as available" without any warranties, express or implied, including but not limited to warranties of merchantability, fitness for a particular purpose, or non-infringement. Users should be aware that the experimental nature of the protocol could lead to unexpected outcomes, including loss of funds.

## III. General Risks

- **Volatility of Cryptocurrency Markets**: The cryptocurrency markets are highly volatile, with prices fluctuating widely in short periods. This volatility can significantly affect the value of assets held within the Euler protocol.

- **Regulatory Risks and Legal Uncertainty**: The legal landscape for DeFi is evolving. Changes in regulations or enforcement actions could impact your ability to use the protocol or result in legal consequences. Users should stay informed about the legal environment and comply with all applicable laws.

- **Limited Liquidity**: The Euler protocol relies on user-provided liquidity in its pools. During periods of high market volatility or when significant withdrawals occur, liquidity within the protocol may be limited. This can disrupt market function, leading to slippage, delays in transaction execution, or the inability to execute trades at desired prices. Users should be aware that limited liquidity could result in financial losses, especially during times of market stress.

## IV. Security Risks

- **Cyberattacks and Hacking**: The Euler protocol, like all DeFi platforms, is susceptible to cyberattacks, hacking, and other security breaches. While we implement rigorous security measures, no system is entirely immune to threats. Risks include, but are not limited to, phishing, malware, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, and other malicious activities.

- **Smart Contract Vulnerabilities**: Despite rigorous testing and auditing, smart contracts may still contain vulnerabilities, such as reentrancy attacks, front-running, timestamp manipulation, and other potential exploits. Users should be aware that exploiting these vulnerabilities could lead to significant financial losses.

- **Nation-State and Black Hat Attacks**: The Euler protocol, like other blockchain systems, may be targeted by highly sophisticated cyberattacks from nation-state actors or black hat hackers. These attackers may exploit known or unknown vulnerabilities, resulting in severe disruptions or loss of assets.

- **Unknown Vulnerabilities**: As technology evolves, new vulnerabilities may be discovered that could affect the security of the Euler protocol. These include potential risks from advances in cryptography, such as quantum computing, which could undermine current blockchain security measures.

- **Risk of Theft**: There is no assurance against the theft of digital assets due to sophisticated cyber-attacks or exploitation of vulnerabilities in the Euler protocol, its underlying blockchain, or associated third-party services. Such incidents could lead to the partial or complete loss of your assets.

- **Service Availability**: The Euler protocol may experience downtime or become inaccessible due to maintenance, third-party service disruptions, or blockchain network issues. Euler does not guarantee continuous availability or functionality of the protocol, and users should be prepared for potential interruptions.

## V. Technical Risks

- **Software Bugs and Technical Failures**: The Euler protocol operates on complex software that may contain bugs or experience failures, potentially leading to loss of funds or interruption of services. These risks are exacerbated by the rapid pace of technological change, which may introduce new vulnerabilities.

- **Impact of Advances in Technology**: Emerging technologies, such as quantum computing, may pose future risks to the security of blockchain-based systems, including the Euler protocol. Users should be aware that such advances could undermine the cryptographic security that the protocol relies upon.

- **Flawed Logic and Design**: The underlying logic and design of the Euler protocol and related software may be flawed, defective, or impaired. This could result in smart contracts operating incorrectly or not as intended, leading to unintended transactions or losses.

## VI. Protocol-Specific Risks

- **Smart Contract Risks**: The Euler protocol's smart contracts are designed to be secure, but they are not infallible. Vulnerabilities may exist, and their exploitation could lead to loss of assets.

- **Functionality and Performance**: There is no guarantee of the continuous, uninterrupted, or error-free operation of the protocol. Users should be aware of potential issues related to its functionality and performance.

## VII. Third-Party Risks

- **Reliance on Third-Party Services and Data**: The Euler protocol may rely on third-party services, oracles, and data feeds. Failures, delays, or inaccuracies in these external systems could negatively impact the protocol's operation or lead to incorrect transaction executions.

- **Risk of Third Parties Acting Late**: Certain functions within the Euler protocol or its underlying blockchain may depend on timely actions by third parties. Delays or failures in these actions could result in inaccessibility of protocol functionality or loss of assets.

**Third-Party Created Vaults:**

- **Risk of Misconfiguration and Malpractice:** The Euler protocol allows for third parties to create and deploy their own vaults using the Euler Vault Kit (EVK). While this fosters innovation and modularity within the ecosystem, it also introduces the risk that some vaults may be misconfigured, improperly governed, or lack the necessary risk controls. Users must be aware that interacting with these third-party-created vaults carries inherent risks. These vaults may not have undergone the expected level of scrutiny, potentially leading to unexpected losses or failures.

- **No Liability for Third-Party Vaults:** Euler disclaims any responsibility or liability for the operation, security, or performance of vaults created by third parties. Users engaging with these vaults do so at their own risk. It is strongly recommended that users perform thorough due diligence on any third-party vault before depositing assets, including reviewing any available documentation, governance structures, and the reputation of the vault creator.
